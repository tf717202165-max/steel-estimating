AI-driven modelling, estimator-led review

AI does the reading, the repetitive modelling and the checking. Each step must pass before the next one starts. The judgement calls stay with me: scope, pricing basis, risk and clarifications.

1 · Read the drawings

Every sheet is read: framing plans, member schedules, sections, connection details and notes. Architecture is used for position, structure for size. Conflicts between them are listed, not guessed.

2 · Plan every member

Each member gets a section, end points, levels and a source: the sheet and the place it was read from, and whether it is confirmed, assumed or a tender allowance.

3 · Build in Tekla Structures

Members, plates and cuts are written into Tekla through its API, zone by zone. A modeller would spend days on this. The AI does it in minutes, and every change is read back and saved.

4 · Check the model

Tekla clash check, supports (nothing stops short or floats), double counts, zero-length parts, and a count of every member type against the drawing schedules.

5 · Estimator review

I review scope, inclusions and exclusions, check the main quantities and turn unclear details into tender clarifications with sheet references.

6 · Issue the pack

Bill of materials (steel by section, plates by thickness, purlins and bridging by length, HD bolt sets), the IFC model and the query list, all from the same model.

My estimating judgement

A fast model only helps if the tender basis is right. This is where experience counts.

Scope review

Read the structure alongside the architecture and the trade interfaces. An unknown quantity is not zero: confirm it, ask for clarification, or qualify it.

Estimate build-up

Main steel by mass, purlins, girts and bridging by length, plates by thickness, hardware by count. Allowances are shown separately, never hidden in the base quantities.

Risks & qualifications

Provisional items, assumed plate sizes and missing details are listed against drawing references. Each one becomes a clear tender clarification.

Revisions

Drawing updates are tracked issue by issue. What changed, where, and by how much, so variations can be priced.
